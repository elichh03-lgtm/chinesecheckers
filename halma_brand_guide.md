# Halma
## Brand Guide — v1.1

---

# 01. Brand Foundation

**Who this brand is**

Halma is not a gaming platform. It is a place where a centuries-old game is played seriously, beautifully, and without compromise. The name is Greek — it means "jump" — and it carries the full weight of that etymology: a decisive action, a commitment, a move from which there is no returning. Halma is what happens when you decide that the way something is done matters as much as whether it's done at all.

Most online game platforms are built for volume. Halma is built for players who would rather spend twenty minutes in a single well-played game than an hour in five careless ones. It asks nothing of the player except their attention. In return, it offers a board that is satisfying to play on, an opponent who has chosen to be here, and a record that says something about both.

The six-pointed star of a Chinese Checkers board — 121 holes, six triangular corners, a hexagonal center — is one of the more beautiful objects in competitive gaming. Halma treats it as such. The marbles are not icons. The board is not a UI element. They are the thing itself, rendered with the care they deserve.

**Mission**

Halma provides a precise, elegant environment for serious play of Chinese Checkers — for anyone willing to show up and move.

**Vision**

To be the definitive place where this game is played online: the reference point against which every other version is measured, and found wanting.

**Core Values**

*Precision.* Halma is exact. Every decision — in the product, in the UI, in the copy — is deliberate. Nothing appears by accident. Nothing is rounded off because rounding off was easier. In practice: if a design element cannot justify its presence, it leaves. If a sentence can be shorter, it is.

*Restraint.* What Halma doesn't do is as meaningful as what it does. No banners, no push notifications, no streaks, no dark patterns for retention. The game is the retention mechanism. In practice: before adding any feature, the question is whether a player who didn't ask for it would notice its absence. If not, it doesn't ship.

*Depth without noise.* The competitive layer — ELO, tiers, history, replay — is there for players who want it, invisible to players who don't. Complexity doesn't shout. In practice: the Jade tier player and the Diamond tier player see the same board.

*The long game.* Halma is not built for the month. It is built for the player who comes back in three years, loads a replay of a game they played in 2026, and remembers what it felt like to move.

**Brand archetype**

Halma operates at the intersection of the Ruler and the Artisan. The Ruler because it holds a standard and does not lower it for comfort. The Artisan because the standard is not about hierarchy or control — it is about doing the thing well, for its own sake. The tension between these two archetypes gives Halma its character: authoritative without being cold, precise without being sterile.

# 02. Positioning

**What we do**

Simple: Halma is where you play Chinese Checkers online.

Complete: Halma is a browser-based platform for real-time multiplayer Chinese Checkers — playable instantly as a guest, or with full competitive infrastructure (ELO rating, ranked matchmaking, game history, replay) for players who want something to work toward.

**Who we serve**

The Halma player is 22–38, plays board games with intention rather than habit, and is mildly annoyed by how most game websites look. They've played Chinese Checkers before — at a family table, on a phone during a flight — and have wondered whether a good version of it exists online. They don't need to be convinced that the game is worth playing. They need to find a place that takes it seriously. They are not the person who needs a tutorial pop-up. They are not the person who needs to be rewarded with a badge for logging in three days in a row. They are the person who will come back because the game is good and the platform doesn't get in the way.

Who is not the Halma player: the person looking for a social network, the person who needs constant feedback loops to stay engaged, the person who wants to customize their avatar with cosmetics they've purchased.

**How we're different**

Against the alternatives — scattered app store implementations, browser versions that look like they were built in 2009, colonist.io and its imitators — Halma does two things that none of them do simultaneously: it takes the visual quality of the game seriously, and it provides the competitive infrastructure that turns a casual game into something worth mastering. The combination is the differentiator.

**Positioning statement**

For competitive board game players who want to play Chinese Checkers properly online, Halma provides a precisely crafted, beautiful competitive environment — unlike gaming platforms that treat the game as a generic product and unlike minimal implementations that offer no reason to return.

**Price/value positioning**

Halma is free to play. The premium offering, when introduced, will be positioned not as "more features" but as support for the platform — the way a reader subscribes to a publication they believe in. The copy around any premium tier will never use the word "unlock."

# 03. Verbal Identity

**Brand voice**

Halma's voice is the written equivalent of a clean, dark room with one good light source. It is spare, exact, and slightly formal — not distant, but not casual either. It does not perform enthusiasm. It does not hedge. It makes a statement and stops.

The tension that gives the voice its character is between authority and simplicity. Halma knows exactly what it is, and chooses to describe it in the shortest possible terms. There is wit in this restraint — the kind of wit that comes from saying less than you could, rather than more.

**Voice in practice**

| Dimension | We are | We are not |
|---|---|---|
| Confidence | Certain, without needing to prove it | Arrogant, boastful, or defensive |
| Warmth | Quiet — present but not performed | Cold, clinical, or indifferent |
| Precision | Exact — one right word, not three approximate ones | Pedantic or over-explained |
| Depth | Dense in meaning, not in word count | Intellectual for its own sake |

**Writing style**

Short sentences. Active voice. No exclamation points — ever. Paragraphs rarely exceed three sentences in marketing contexts; one sentence is not too few.

Tone shifts by context: on the landing page, the copy is the most reduced — almost aphoristic. In email, slightly warmer, but never chatty. In UI copy, pure function — label what the action does, nothing else. Error messages acknowledge the error once and tell the player what to do next.

No phrasing that could appear on any other gaming platform: "ready to play?", "level up your game", "your adventure starts here." These are banned not because they're wrong but because they're not Halma.

**Tagline**

Primary: *The board, and nothing more.*
Variant (competitive): *Play it properly.*
Variant (platform/ethos): *Where the game begins.*

**Messaging pillars**

*The board is the thing.* Everything Halma does serves the game. The UI does not compete with the board for attention. The brand does not compete with the game for identity. The board is a 121-hole, six-cornered precision object, and it deserves a platform that treats it as one. Use when: describing the visual and product experience, differentiating from cluttered competitors.

*Mastery is available.* The ELO system, ranked tiers, move history, and replay are not for elite players — they are for any player who wants to know how they're doing and where they can improve. Depth doesn't require gatekeeping. Use when: explaining the competitive layer, introducing ranked features.

*No friction.* Guest play requires no account. A game starts in seconds. Nothing is required of the player except showing up. The platform works and then gets out of the way. Use when: describing zero-friction entry, addressing signup hesitation.

*Every game stays.* Every ranked and custom game is stored as a complete move log, shareable replay, and permanent record. This is not a statistical nicety — it is a commitment to the seriousness of each game played. Watching a replay of a well-contested match is part of what Halma offers. Use when: describing the replay feature, differentiating from ephemeral implementations, positioning the history and spectator systems.

**Naming convention**

Halma is the platform name. The game is referred to as "Chinese Checkers" in all player-facing contexts — never "Chinese Checkers Online," never "Halma Game." The brand name and game name coexist clearly: *Halma. Chinese Checkers, played properly.*

"Sternhalma" — the historically accurate German name — may appear once, on a dedicated about or history page, for players who know it. It is never used in navigation, onboarding, or marketing copy. It is a note of credibility, not a label.

**Elevator pitches**

Casual: "It's Chinese Checkers online — clean, fast, competitive if you want it to be."

Formal: "Halma is a browser-based platform for multiplayer Chinese Checkers. Play instantly as a guest, or create an account for ELO ranking, match history, and replay. The emphasis is on the quality of the game and the experience of playing it — not on everything around it."

# 04. Visual Identity

**Color system**

| Name | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Void | #080808 | Primary background — page, modal, overlay |
| Surface | #141414 | Card backgrounds, panel surfaces |
| Raised | #1A1A1A | Elevated surfaces, hover states |
| Border | #242424 | Dividers, input borders, separators |
| Muted | #505050 | Disabled states, placeholder text |
| Secondary | #888888 | Secondary text, captions, labels |
| Primary | #F5F5F5 | Primary text, headlines |

**Tier accent colors** — used only in ranking and gameplay contexts

| Tier | Name | Hex | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jade | Teal green | #1B9E6E | Shifted from Emerald marble (#2EA043) to avoid ambiguity |
| Pearl | Pearl white | #E8E8E0 | No marble overlap |
| Coral | Brick red | #C03A2B | Shifted from Ruby marble (#E84040) to avoid ambiguity |
| Amber | Amber gold | #F59E0B | No marble overlap |
| Obsidian | Deep violet | #5B4FCF | Shifted from Violet marble (#8B5CF6) to avoid ambiguity |
| Diamond | Ice blue | #A8D8F0 | No marble overlap |

Tier accent colors are deliberately offset from the six marble colors used during gameplay (#E84040, #4A9EFF, #2EA043, #F0B429, #8B5CF6, #F97316). A player's ranking display must never read as a continuation of their marble color.

**Color rules**

The UI palette is monochrome. Color enters the brand only through gameplay and ranking contexts — tier badges, marble colors, the board's active state. On any screen that isn't an active game or a ranking display, the only colors are shades of black, gray, and white.

The jade green tone specified as a primary UI accent in the product design system (#2EA043) is superseded by this rule. UI interactive states — primary buttons, active navigation, focus rings, hover states — use white and gray values only. Jade enters the UI exclusively as the Jade tier badge and as on-board active-state highlights during gameplay. Nowhere else.

Never introduce color for decoration. The landing hero is monochrome. The lobby is monochrome. The profile page is monochrome except for the tier badge. This makes color meaningful when it appears.

Forbidden: blue hyperlinks, green success states in brand contexts, any use of marble or tier colors in navigation, headers, or marketing copy outside of their specific contexts.

**Typography**

*Bodoni Moda 900* — Display, headlines, wordmark. The maximum weight creates the thick-thin stroke contrast that defines the font. Set with negative tracking (−0.02em to −0.03em) at display sizes. Optical sizing enabled. This is not a font that apologizes for itself.

*Jost 300* — Body copy, UI labels, secondary copy. Geometric, airy, modern. The lightness of Jost against Bodoni 900 is the core typographic tension of the brand: the exact opposite of the display font in weight, spirit, and historical period. This contrast is intentional and must be maintained.

*IBM Plex Mono 400* — Data, labels, metadata, timestamps, ELO numbers, move counts, coordinate displays. Signals precision and editorial seriousness. Used wherever a number or technical datum appears. Never bold.

**Type scale**

| Role | Size | Weight | Font |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display XL | 72px | 900 | Bodoni Moda |
| Display | 48px | 900 | Bodoni Moda |
| Headline | 32px | 900 | Bodoni Moda |
| Subhead | 18px | 300 | Jost |
| Body | 16px | 300 | Jost |
| Label | 13px | 400 | Jost |
| Caption / Mono | 12px | 400 | IBM Plex Mono |

**Type rules**

Headlines set in Bodoni Moda use negative tracking (−0.02em to −0.03em). Body text in Jost uses normal or very slightly positive tracking (+0.01em) for readability. IBM Plex Mono labels are never bold.

Never set body copy in Bodoni Moda. Never set headlines in IBM Plex Mono. Italic Bodoni (regular weight, optical sizing) is used sparingly — for tier names, game titles, editorial pull quotes — as a gesture, not a default.

**Logo direction**

The Halma wordmark is set in Bodoni Moda 900, tracked at −0.03em, sentence case. No icon, no symbol, no monogram — the wordmark is the mark. The geometry of the letters carries the identity: the structural H, the crossbar of the A echoing the board's triangular grid.

Secondary form: a standalone "H" in Bodoni 900, used as favicon or app icon only. Never distort, recolor, or apply the wordmark on surfaces that introduce color behind it. White on Void, always.

**Photography and imagery**

When photography is used: close, still, specific. A marble resting in its hole. A hand mid-move. The board at oblique angle, tight depth of field. Light is directional and clean — not warm, not dramatic. Grain, if any, is minimal.

Halma does not use gaming stock photography: no headsets, no glowing keyboards, no people celebrating at screens. The game is between two people and a board. Photography reflects this.

**Design principles**

*Type as layout.* The headline is the visual. Halma pages do not require hero images, illustrations, or decorative elements to communicate. A Bodoni 900 headline at 72px, set against Void, is complete.

*Extreme negative space.* Margins are generous. Content does not fill available space — it occupies the space it needs, and no more. Density is not a virtue.

*The game is the color.* The UI palette is monochrome. Color is reserved for gameplay and ranking. Its scarcity makes it meaningful.

*Hard edges.* Halma does not use rounded corners on primary brand elements — cards, inputs, modals, and frames use `border-radius: 0` or `2px` maximum. Rounded corners signal approachability; Halma signals precision. This applies to UI chrome only. Game elements — board holes, marbles, selection rings — are circular by nature and are exempt.

*Monospace for data.* Every number that appears in the UI — ELO ratings, move counts, timestamps, win rates — is set in IBM Plex Mono. Numbers are data. Data has a typeface.

# 05. Vocabulary Guide

**Words native to this brand**

| Word | Why it fits |
|---|---|
| Precise | Describes both the game and the brand's approach without inflation |
| Play | Simple, complete — "play" not "game" or "compete" |
| Move | The fundamental unit of the game; used for actions broadly |
| Board | The object — never "game board" or "playing surface" |
| Ranked | Denotes the competitive tier without gamification baggage |
| History | More weight than "stats" — implies narrative, not just data |
| Replay | A specific, earned term for the move-log review feature |
| Position | Where a marble sits; used for competitive standing as well |
| Tier | Clean, without gamification connotation |
| Craft | For describing skill development — not "grinding" |

**Words to avoid**

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Amazing / awesome | [omit, or be specific] |
| Game-changer | [never] |
| Level up | Improve, progress, advance |
| Adventure / journey | [omit] |
| Leaderboard | Rankings, tier standings |
| Challenge | Game, match |
| Unlock | [rephrase the sentence entirely] |
| Engage | Play |
| Experience | [be specific about what the experience is] |
| Community | Players (unless describing something specific) |

**Guest player names**

Guest players are assigned names in the format "Marble·[4-digit number]" — for example, Marble·4821 or Marble·0093. The marble reference acknowledges the game object without appending a color prefix (no "TealMarble," "RedMarble"). The number is a serial, not a username. Guests who register may replace this with a chosen display name.

**Tier indicators**

Tiers are displayed as an 8px filled circle in the tier's accent color, set inline before the tier name in Bodoni italic. No emoji. No gamification badge graphics. The indicator is a color and a word — nothing more.

**Phrases that sound like this brand**

- "The board is ready."
- "Play it properly."
- "Your history, move by move."
- "Ranked play. No shortcuts."
- "Halma. Chinese Checkers, played properly."
- "A guest game takes eight seconds to start."
- "Every game stays."
- "The board, and nothing more."

**Phrases that don't**

- "Ready to challenge yourself?"
- "Join the Halma community today!"
- "Your epic journey starts here."
- "Level up and dominate the board!"
- "We think you'll love this."
- "Amazing news — ranked play is here!"

# 06. Brand Applications

**Website**

The landing page hero is a single Bodoni 900 headline — "Halma." or "Play." — against Void background. Below it: one sentence of body copy in Jost 300. Below that: two buttons — "Play as guest" and "Sign in." No explanation of what Chinese Checkers is on the hero. The player who doesn't know will scroll; the player who does knows immediately they're in the right place.

CTAs are verbs. "Play." "Sign in." "View replay." Never "Get started" or "Join now."

Pricing, if introduced, is presented as a single number with one sentence of context. No feature comparison grids. No "most popular" badges.

**Email**

Subject lines are statements, not questions. "Your ranking, updated." not "How did you do this week?" Body copy is short — three sentences maximum for transactional emails. Tone is slightly warmer than the website but still spare. No promotional language in transactional emails. No cross-sell.

**In-game and UI copy**

Pure function. "Your turn." not "It's your turn to make a move!" Error states: "Invalid move." not "Oops! That move isn't allowed." Button labels are single words where possible: "Resign." "Rematch." "Leave."

The one exception: the end-game state. Win and loss messages may be one sentence longer. "You finished first. Elo updated." is complete. "You finished third. The move log is saved." is enough.

**Social**

Halma's social presence, if any, documents play rather than promoting the platform. Interesting board positions. Notable games. Milestone ELO achievements shared with the player's permission. No promotional copy, no sale announcements, no engagement bait.

Account bio, wherever Halma appears: "Chinese Checkers, played properly." One sentence. No emoji.

**Lobby and waiting room**

The lobby browser is a sparse list — room name, host, player count, timer setting — rendered in Jost 300 with settings values in IBM Plex Mono. No pressure copy, no "hurry — filling fast" language. The one action is "Join." Spectator count, where visible, appears as "4 watching" in IBM Plex Mono at secondary weight. Nothing about the spectators' presence is celebrated or announced.

**Pre-game room**

The waiting room copy is minimal. "Waiting for players." as the state. Player names appear as they join — no animation, no chime, no "PlayerName has entered." The names appear. The room fills. When it's ready: "Game starting." Then it starts.

**Spectator experience**

Spectators see the full board in real time and are acknowledged once — a small IBM Plex Mono count in the corner ("3 watching"), at #505050. They are not addressed, not prompted, not given special onboarding. Their presence is noted and then respected. Chat from spectators is distinguished visually (italicised, slightly muted) but not demoted — they are part of the game's record.

**In person**

Halma describes itself in one sentence. It does not use the word "exciting." If pressed for more: "The competitive layer — ELO, ranked tiers, replay — is there for players who want it. The game itself is there for everyone." Then stops.

# 07. What Halma Is Never

**Loud.** Halma does not use exclamation points, all-caps urgency, or visual noise to communicate importance. If something matters, it is stated plainly. When a brand has to shout to be heard, it has already failed. Halma would rather be missed by the wrong player than found by the wrong one.

**Gamified.** Halma does not have daily streaks, login rewards, achievement badges for completing a tutorial, or any mechanism that substitutes the feeling of progress for actual progress. The ELO system is a measurement, not a reward. Players improve because they play well, not because they logged in seven days in a row.

**Apologetic.** Halma does not hedge. It does not say "we think" or "we believe" when it means to make a statement. It does not soften product decisions with qualifications. The platform has a point of view. That point of view is expressed directly.

**Cluttered.** Every element on every screen has a reason to exist. Decoration is not a reason. Visual complexity does not signal quality — it signals insecurity. When in doubt, less.

**Designed for distraction.** Halma does not send notifications that weren't requested. It does not surface content to keep players on the platform beyond the game they came to play. It does not send emails that weren't earned by a specific action. It respects that the player's attention is finite and valuable.

**Generic.** "Join thousands of players" is not a Halma sentence. The brand does not cite user counts as a credibility signal. The game is good. The platform serves it well. These are the arguments. They are sufficient.

**Warm toward everyone.** Halma is not for every player. A player who wants tutorials, social feeds, cosmetic unlocks, and a cheering interface will be better served by something else. Halma is for players who want to play the game. This is not a failure of the brand — it is the brand.
